Why this workshop

Life organizes itself, at every scale.

From cytoskeletal filaments to fish schools, living matter routinely produces coordinated, adaptive behavior without central control. This workshop brings together physicists, biologists, ecologists, and neuroscientists who study how local interactions between many simple units — cells, organisms, neurons — give rise to robust collective phenomena at the scale above them.

Over three days of talks, posters, and open discussion sessions, we'll ask what unifying principles, if any, connect a slime mold solving a maze, a starling murmuration evading a hawk, and a cortical network encoding a decision.

Themes & topics

Six threads run through the program, spanning wet-lab biology, field ecology, and theoretical physics.

PHYS

Active matter & swarming

Self-propelled particles, flocking transitions, and the statistical physics of collective motion.

BIO

Morphogenesis & pattern formation

How cell collectives build reproducible tissue-scale structure from local signaling rules.

ECO

Social insects & colony behavior

Division of labor, stigmergy, and decision-making in ant, bee, and termite colonies.

NEURO

Neural population dynamics

Coordinated activity across neurons as a collective computation problem.

EPI

Spreading processes

Epidemics, information cascades, and opinion dynamics on networks of interacting agents.

MATH

Theory & inference

Data-driven models for inferring interaction rules from collective trajectories.
